# Sources and factual notes

## Topic

Could an AI influencer sell your product? — an explainer short about AI-generated
influencers entering marketing.

## Sources

1. **Higgsfield — New AI influencer**
   https://higgsfield.ai/blog/new-ai-influencer
   Vendor blog describing an AI influencer tool. Used only as an example of the
   tooling category, not as evidence of business results.

2. **Semafor — Creative tech startup Higgsfield launches AI influencer tool**
   https://www.semafor.com/article/10/07/2026/creative-tech-startup-higgsfield-launches-ai-influencer-tool
   Reporting on the launch of an AI influencer tool.

3. **Fast Company — She has 400,000 Instagram followers and major brand deals. She's also AI.**
   https://www.fastcompany.com/91546466/she-has-400000-instagram-followers-and-major-brand-deals-shes-also-ai
   Profile of Aitana Lopez, a virtual creator. This is the only named example used
   in the video, and it is presented exactly as the source does.

4. **The Guardian — Brands using AI-generated influencers to promote products on social media**
   https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/jun/21/brands-using-ai-generated-influencers-to-promote-products-on-social-media
   Reporting on brands using AI-generated influencers.

5. **FTC — Disclosures 101 for social media influencers**
   https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers
   Official guidance on disclosing material connections. Basis for the
   transparency takeaway.

## What the video does NOT claim

Deliberately avoided, per the brief:

- No claim that virtual influencers **guarantee sales**.
- No claim that they **reduce marketing costs by a specific percentage**.
- No claim that they **outperform human creators**.
- No invented engagement rates, follower counts, conversion rates or testimonials.
- No fabricated business results of any kind.

## On-screen claims actually made

| Claim | Basis |
|---|---|
| AI influencers attract audiences and land brand deals | Sources 3 and 4 |
| Aitana Lopez is a virtual creator with hundreds of thousands of followers | Source 3 (Fast Company) |
| Tools let businesses create a digital character and place it in product videos | Sources 1 and 2 |
| Attention does not guarantee sales | General point; no statistic cited |
| A fictional character presented as a real customer can mislead viewers | Source 5 (FTC disclosure guidance) |

## Imagery

All visuals are code-generated (React/CSS). The character is an abstract geometric
construction, not a likeness of any real person. Product props are generic and
unbranded. No stock footage, image-generation APIs or external AI video services
were used.
